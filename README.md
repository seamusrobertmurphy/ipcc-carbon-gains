# ipcc-carbon-gains

**Counting the gains: IPCC Tier 1 and Tier 2 removal pathways in peatlands, coastal wetlands and improved grasslands.**

A quantified synthesis of the carbon **gains** that jurisdictions and project developers are now permitted to count under IPCC guidance, and what each is worth per hectare per year.

Status: design stage, analysis in progress. Sole-authored. Target venue **Carbon Balance and Management** (guidelines not yet verified against the live journal pages).

---

## The problem

The gain pathways exist, but they are scattered across two supplements, five chapters and roughly fifteen tables, with duration rules that contradict one another and no greenhouse-gas-weighted aggregation anywhere. A developer wanting to know what a rewetted fen or a replanted mangrove is actually worth has to reconstruct it from primary documents. Assembling that into one coherent accounting framework is the contribution.

The pivotal sentence is the IPCC's own, in Chapter 3 of the 2013 Wetlands Supplement. Its Equation 3.1 replaces Equations 2.24 and 2.26 of the 2006 Guidelines because those:

> implicitly assumed that organic soils can only lose carbon, while in fact undrained or rewetted organic soils can accumulate soil organic carbon if covered with vegetation.

That change produced the first negative Tier 1 emission factors in the history of the guidelines. Peatlands, coastal wetlands and improved grasslands stopped being sources to be avoided and became pools that can be credited for growth.

## What is countable, and what it is worth

Tier 1 defaults. Removals are shown as positive gains.

| Pathway | Source | Gain (t CO2/ha/yr) | Runs for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mangrove rewetting, revegetation or creation, soil | 2013 WS Table 4.12 | **5.94** | Until the 386 t C/ha ceiling, roughly 240 years |
| Mangrove biomass | 2013 WS Tables 4.3 to 4.5 | 9.9 t d.m./ha/yr, ~129 t C/ha total | ~19 years to stock ceiling |
| Tidal marsh soil | 2013 WS Table 4.12 | **3.34** | As above |
| Seagrass meadow soil | 2013 WS Table 4.12 | 1.58 (n = 6) | As above |
| Rewetted boreal organic soil, nutrient-rich | 2013 WS Table 3.1 | **2.02** removal | From year 1, no transition period |
| Rewetting, net benefit against drained counterfactual | 2013 WS Ch. 2 vs Ch. 3 | 1.35 to **52.4** t CO2e | Indefinite |
| Improved grassland, nominal to improved + high input | 2019 Table 6.2 | 0.92 to **6.71** | 20 years |
| Improved grassland, severely degraded to improved + high | 2019 Table 6.2 | 1.97 to **14.30** | 20 years |
| Inland mineral wetland, rewetting | 2013 WS Table 5.3 | 3.4, but methane costs 5.9, so a **net source** | 40 years, uniquely |
| Biochar in mineral soils | 2019 Equation 2.25A | New pathway, Tier 2 | n/a |

Grassland figures span climate zones and soil classes; the full matrix is generated by the pipeline.

## Three findings that reframe the opportunity

1. **The upgrade is 2013, not 2019.** The Wetlands Supplement created the countable organic-soil gain. The 2019 Refinement left peatlands untouched: its own Annex 1 states "There are no refinements to the sections on peatlands."

2. **The 2019 Refinement made mineral-soil gains smaller.** Its Overview says "Many of the updated factors reflect a smaller impact of anthropogenic activity on soil carbon than default factors provided in the 2006 IPCC Guidelines." No-till fell from 1.10 to 1.22 down to 1.03 to 1.10, roughly halving that gain, and reference stocks fell alongside it. Grassland is the weaker half of the story and wetlands the stronger, which is the reverse of how this is usually pitched.

3. **Coastal wetlands escape the 20-year cap.** Table 4.12's factor applies "as long as the soil remains rewetted and vegetated", until stocks reach the natural ceiling. Every other soil pathway is capped at 20 years, and inland mineral wetlands at 40. Two gates apply: the vegetation must be **planted or seeded**, since natural recolonisation scores zero, and above 18 ppt salinity methane is zero by assumption, while below it costs 193.7 kg CH4/ha/yr and can turn the project into a source.

## What the guidance does not give you

Stated plainly, because each is a gap the paper has to fill or flag.

- No global warming potential weighting anywhere in the 354 pages of the Wetlands Supplement. No CO2-equivalent aggregation, no time-to-break-even for the methane penalty on rewetting.
- No Tier 1 accumulation rate for undrained, natural, managed peatland. A tropical undrained sink of 0.3 to 1.1 t CO2-C/ha/yr appears in annex prose and is deliberately not promoted to an emission factor.
- No published water-table-to-flux regression coefficients, although the regressions were fitted and plotted.
- No climate-zone disaggregation of the coastal accumulation factors at Tier 1.
- No guidance for restoration or rehabilitation that does not involve rewetting.

## Two corrections carried in the record

`IPCC-2010-Erata-Land-Climate-Soil-Carbon-Classification.pdf` is **not an IPCC document and not an erratum**. It is EU Commission Decision 2010/335/EU under the Renewable Energy Directive, and its Table 1 is a verbatim copy of IPCC 2006 Table 2.3. The real 2010 event is the IPCC Expert Meeting in Geneva that October, which is what led to the Wetlands Supplement.

There was never an immediate-100%-oxidation default for **soil** carbon. The 20-year linear transition dates to the 1996 Guidelines and was restated in 2003 and 2006. The immediate-loss assumption applies to **biomass**.
