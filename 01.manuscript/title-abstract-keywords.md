## Title

**Net greenhouse gas balance of IPCC carbon gain pathways in peatlands, coastal wetlands and
improved grasslands: magnitude, duration and metric dependence, applied to Ireland**

173 characters. Within the journal's observed range.

Two deliberate omissions. "Tier 1 and Tier 2" is dropped from the title and carried in the
abstract's first line, because the ecosystem names earn more discoverability than the tier
designation, which is jargon to anyone outside inventory work. And the finding is not stated
declaratively, matching the journal's descriptive house style rather than the Nature-family
convention that dominates this literature.

**Shorter fallback if an editor asks** (155 characters), which keeps the tiers but loses the
ecosystems:

> Net greenhouse gas balance of IPCC Tier 1 and Tier 2 soil carbon gain pathways: magnitude,
> duration and metric dependence, with a worked example for Ireland

**Caution.** "Metric dependence" asserts that the answer changes with the metric. Zhang et
al. (2025) found for UK peatlands that it changes the confidence but not the headline
conclusion. If the results come out that way, soften to "metric sensitivity", which promises
a sensitivity analysis rather than a dependence finding.

## Abstract

301 words. **Square brackets mark quantities the analysis has not yet produced. Do not
submit with brackets remaining, and do not fill them with anything but computed results.**

> **Background.** The 2013 IPCC Wetlands Supplement replaced Equations 2.24 and 2.26 of the
> 2006 Guidelines because those "implicitly assumed that organic soils can only lose
> carbon", introducing the first negative Tier 1 emission factors in the guidelines'
> history. Carbon gains in peatlands, coastal wetlands and improved grasslands are
> consequently countable in national greenhouse gas inventories and creditable in principle.
> The pathways are distributed across two supplements and five chapters, carry duration
> rules differing by more than two orders of magnitude, and are nowhere aggregated on a
> greenhouse-gas-weighted basis.
>
> **Results.** We assemble [n] countable Tier 1 and Tier 2 gain pathways with their
> magnitude, duration rule, eligibility gate and non-CO2 offset, each traced to a numbered
> IPCC table or equation. Tier 1 gains range from 0.92 to 14.30 t CO2 ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ for improved
> grassland and reach 5.94 t CO2 ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ for mangrove soil. Duration rules span from the
> year of rewetting without limit, through 20 and 40 years, to a stock ceiling roughly 240
> years distant. Under GWP-100, GWP-20 and GWP\*, [k] of [n] pathway-condition combinations
> return a net greenhouse gas source despite a compliant, countable CO2 gain. Inland mineral
> wetland rewetting is a net source at Tier 1 defaults. Improved grassland fertilised at
> 100 kg N ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ on low-carbon soils crosses to a net source at year [31]. Applied to
> Ireland, where drained nutrient-rich peat underlies intensively fertilised grassland,
> [result].
>
> **Conclusions.** A countable carbon gain is not equivalent to a climate benefit. Duration
> rules and non-CO2 offsets, rather than the headline emission factor, determine whether a
> pathway delivers mitigation, and the ranking of pathways is [in]sensitive to metric choice.
> Inventory compilers and crediting programmes should report the offsetting fluxes and the
> applicable duration rule alongside any claimed soil carbon gain.
